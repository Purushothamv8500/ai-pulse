Product Name

AI Pulse

Tagline

Know what happened. Understand why it matters. Learn what comes next.

1. ROLE

You are the lead product architect, senior UX/UI designer, senior full-stack engineer, AI engineer, database architect, DevOps engineer, QA engineer, security engineer, and technical project manager for this project.

You must approach this project as if you are building a production-grade SaaS product, not a simple demo.

Do not immediately start writing large amounts of code.

First understand the complete product architecture, requirements, user flows, database design, APIs, AI pipeline, ingestion pipeline, security requirements, deployment architecture, and development phases.

Whenever a requirement is ambiguous, identify the ambiguity and propose the best production-grade solution.

Do not make unnecessary architectural decisions without explaining the trade-off.

2. PRODUCT VISION

Build a personalized AI intelligence and learning platform called AI Pulse.

The problem:

AI is evolving extremely quickly.

Users currently need to spend significant amounts of time checking:

AI news
Research papers
AI company announcements
New AI models
Benchmark results
Open-source projects
AI tools
Developer announcements
Technical articles
Tutorials
Industry developments

Most users do not have enough time to continuously monitor all of these sources.

AI Pulse should solve this problem.

The application should automatically collect important AI developments, analyze them using AI, rank their importance, summarize them, explain why they matter, and tell the user what they should learn next.

The core philosophy is:

Do not simply summarize AI news.

Instead:

Discover → Filter → Understand → Learn

The product should answer three questions:

What happened?
Why does it matter?
What should I learn next?
3. CORE VALUE PROPOSITION

AI Pulse should save users time while helping them continuously improve their AI knowledge.

The primary daily experience should be:

"Give me the 5–10 AI developments I actually need to know today, explain them clearly, tell me why they matter, and recommend what I should learn next."

8. "WHY SHOULD I CARE?" FEATURE

This is one of the most important features.

Never create a simple generic news summary.

For every important item answer:

"What changes because of this?"

Examples:

Instead of:

"Company X released a new model."

Say:

"This matters because the model improves performance in coding tasks while reducing inference cost. Developers building coding assistants may be able to use a smaller model while maintaining similar quality."

The system should prioritize practical understanding.
